THE HISTORY
OF FORMULA 1

Scroll down to ride through the eras ↓

THE ORIGINS (1950s)

Formula One racing officially began in 1950 with the inaugural World Championship at Silverstone. The early era was dominated by legendary manufacturers like Alfa Romeo, Ferrari, and Maserati. Drivers like Juan Manuel Fangio set the early benchmark in a time when racing was incredibly raw.

THE AERODYNAMIC ERA (1970s)

The introduction of ground-effect aerodynamics revolutionized the sport. Cars began generating massive amounts of downforce, sucking them to the track and allowing for unprecedented cornering speeds. This era saw fierce rivalries, most notably between Niki Lauda and James Hunt.

TECHNOLOGICAL WARFARE (1990s)

Electronic driver aids, active suspension, and incredibly complex V10 engines defined this era. Michael Schumacher and Ferrari formed an unstoppable dynasty, while safety became a paramount focus following the tragic weekend at Imola in 1994.

THE HYBRID ERA (2014 - Present)

F1 entered its most advanced phase with 1.6-liter V6 turbo-hybrid power units. These engines are marvels of engineering, achieving over 50% thermal efficiency and producing over 1000 horsepower, leading to intense battles between Red Bull Racing, Ferrari, and McLaren.
